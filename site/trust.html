Trust & privacy

Built to be worth trusting.

Two hard questions come up in every serious conversation about Givework: who's accountable for the work, and where does a nonprofit's information actually go? Here are the plain answers — enforced in open-source code, not promises.

Where your information goes

Redacted first. Reviewed always.

01

Your email arrives — from a sender we know

Requests are only accepted from verified partner organizations, over authenticated email. Strangers can't feed work into the system, and attachments are never stored.

02

Personal details are replaced before anything else sees them

Email addresses, phone numbers, and ID or card numbers are swapped for placeholders like [EMAIL_1] the moment a request arrives. The key to those placeholders never leaves our systems.

03

A person reviews before any work starts

Every request is checked by a human before it becomes tasks. Anything that looks like health data is flagged automatically and stopped — we don't accept medical information at all.

04

Only trusted volunteers, then back to you

Sensitive work is limited to volunteers with a verified identity who have signed a data-handling agreement. When results come back, the real details are restored — visible only to you.

The guarantees

Enforced in code, not in fine print.

Redaction at the front door

Structured personal details are tokenized before task text is drafted — volunteers only ever see placeholders. A second sweep runs before anything is published.

No health data — a bright line

Givework isn't built for medical information, so we refuse it: requests that look like they contain health data are flagged and blocked until a person has reviewed them.

A human gate on every request

No email becomes work without a person reviewing it, and results are delivered by us — never directly from a volunteer to you.

Verified, signed volunteers

Non-public work requires a verified identity and a signed volunteer agreement. Both are checked at the moment a task is claimed, so revoking either takes effect instantly.

Attachments aren't stored

Files sent to intake are noted but never saved to our systems — document contents can't leak from a place they never reached.

Open source, end to end

Every mechanism on this page is public code you can read, from the redaction pass to the budget ledger. Don't take our word for any of it.

Accountability

Who stands behind the work.

01

Volunteers donate computing, not services

A volunteer lends spare capacity from a subscription they already pay for — like donating processor time to science projects. They never talk to nonprofits, never see who a task is for, and owe no one a deliverable.

02

Givework is the counterparty

We break the request into tasks, review the results, and deliver them under our own terms. If something's wrong, you talk to us — there is no volunteer to chase, and no volunteer on the hook.

03

You are always the final reviewer

Everything you receive is a draft for a person on your side to check before use. We don't accept requests that call for regulated professional advice — legal, medical, or financial judgment stays with humans you choose.

What we don't claim

The honest edges.

Volunteer machines really do run the work — that's the whole model — so we'll never pretend your text never touches one. That's exactly why details are redacted before tasks are drafted, why sensitive work needs a signed agreement, and why we ask you to send the minimum a task needs. Automatic redaction catches structured details reliably; it can't catch every name in free-flowing text — which is why a person reviews every request too, and why the truly sensitive stuff shouldn't be sent to anyone, including us.
